The **Lunar Lander Neutron and Dosimetry (LND)** experiment, built at Kiel University, flies on China's Chang'E-4 lander — the first spacecraft to land on the far side of the Moon (January 2019). LND measures charged particles, neutrons and radiation dose on the lunar surface, which is essential for planning human exploration.

## My role
Primary scientist for LND operations and data analysis at Kiel, and **project manager from 2019 to 2023**. I calibrated LND's data products with Geant4 simulations and led several of its first science results.

## Key results
- **First solar energetic particles on the lunar far side** — identified a widespread but impulsive SEP event in LND data ([ApJL 2020](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/abbccc)).
- **Primary and albedo protons** — first measurement of primary (GCR) and albedo proton spectra on the lunar surface during the solar minimum between cycles 24 and 25 ([Front. Astron. Space Sci. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspas.2022.974946)).
- **A galactic cosmic-ray cavity in Earth-Moon space** — LND revealed ~20% fewer GCR protons in part of the Moon's orbit, where Earth's magnetosphere shadows incoming cosmic rays; co-first author ([Science Advances 2026](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adv1908)).
- Contributions to the first radiation-dose measurement on the Moon (Science Advances 2020) and the first ground-level enhancement seen on Earth, Moon and Mars (GRL 2023).

## Today
I continue to mentor student projects on LND SEP data at Caltech.
